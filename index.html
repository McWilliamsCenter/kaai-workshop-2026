Overview

About the workshop

Astrophysics and cosmology increasingly rely on large-scale simulations to connect theory with noisy, high-dimensional, unstructured observations. Recently, considerable interest has turned toward foundation models, which learn shared, transferable representations from large and heterogeneous datasets, a natural fit for representing, compressing, and analyzing astrophysical data. In addition, agentic methods are beginning to reshape parts of the scientific process itself, from experiment design to analysis, raising open questions about how such tools can responsibly augment astronomical research. Both directions face real challenges: foundation models are limited by scarce ground-truth data and simulations, while agentic methods must contend with tasks that are often non-verifiable or computationally expensive. This workshop brings together researchers working on both fronts to discuss these challenges and where the field is headed.

  • Foundation models for astrophysics: representing, manipulating, compressing, comparing, and interpreting astrophysical data, from simulations to observations.
  • Agentic science loops: how agentic methods can responsibly augment the astronomical research process.
